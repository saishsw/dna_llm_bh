What is happening in the tube

The model. A small neural network that reads the last 6 letters of Shakespeare and guesses the next one. All it knows is 7,457 numbers, its weights.

Weights become molecules. Each weight is a short DNA strand mixed into a tube. A bigger weight means more of that strand. The input letters are strands too.

Chemistry does the math. Strands pair up and knock each other loose in a reaction called strand displacement. The amount of output strand that comes out equals the weighted sum a computer would calculate.

Light reads the answer. Output strands carry fluorescent tags. A plate reader measures the glow, and the brightest output is the predicted letter.

6 letters→input strands→weight gates→seesaw gates→output strands→plate reader→next letter

Real chemistry is imperfect: pipettes are off by a few percent, some reactions fire on their own, and fuel runs out. Every slider on this page is one of those imperfections. The key at the bottom defines every term, and the i buttons beside the controls explain each one in place.

    What this can and cannot claim

    Constants are from the literature, not a bench. σ, leak, 20 kT per displacement, settling times, the 50 W plate reader and the 103–104 domain library are published values. The shape of the frontier and the ordering of effects are the defensible claims; absolute joules are not.

    Built networks hold 102–103 weights. Even the 4-unit model here (961 weights, about 1.1k strands) is at the edge of what has been demonstrated. Width 32 is a projection; anything over 104 strands is extrapolation.

    Batch amortisation is the fragile number. It assumes one molecule per species per inference and 6×1013 independent reaction volumes. Move copies per inference to 100 and the bulk tube loses to a GPU running the same model.

    This page is the same simulator. sim.js reproduces dna.py to within 2×10−6 relative perplexity on deterministic recipes. Pipetting draws use a different random generator, so a preparation here is not the same draw as a PyTorch seed.

    Model: one-hot context of 6 characters × 33 symbols → hidden layer → 33 logits, trained in FP32 on tiny Shakespeare. Source: BASES_Project (dna.py, model.py, sweep.py).
